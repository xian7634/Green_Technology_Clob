DEFINITION

What is Sustainable Technologies?

Sustainable technology refers to a technological system that minimises negative environmental impacts, conserves natural resources, and promotes long-term social and economic well-being throughout its entire lifecycle. Its core principle is to meet the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

Triple Bottom Line Value:

1. Oriented to environmental, reduce polutions of human activities.

2. Oriented to economic, its development should not rely too heavily on subsidies.

3. Oriented to social, its should be equity and accessibility.

Green Buildings

Green buildings are structures that, throughout their entire lifecycle (planning, design, construction, operation, and demolition), maximize resource c onservation (energy, land, water, materials), reduce pollution, and provide occupants with healthy, comfortable, and efficient spaces.

Why is it needed?:

The global building sector accounts for over 30% of energy consumption. Green buildings can significantly reduce operational carbon emissions and improve indoor air quality.

Precision Farming

Smart agriculture utilizes the Internet of Things (IoT), big data, Artificial Intelligence (AI), and remote sensing technologies to precisely monitor, control, a nd make decisions for agricultural production, aiming to minimize resource inputs and maximize outputs.

Why is it needed?:

Traditional agriculture consumes 70% of the world's freshwater. Smart agriculture can save over 50% of water usage and reduce excessive fertilizer and pesticide application

Recycling Technology

Recycling technology refers to the processes that convert waste (solid, liquid, or gas) into reusable raw materials or energy through physical, chemical, or biological methods, aiming to build a closed-loop circular economy.

Why is it needed?:

Reduces dependence on virgin mineral resources and mitigates environmental pollution caused by landfilling and incineration.

Energy-Efficient Technology

Energy-efficient technologies reduce total energy consumption through technical means while meeting the same level of demand, aim to doing more with lesser energy.

Why is it needed?:

Reduce energy consumption directly equates to reducing fossil fuel combustion, making it the most cost-effective emission reduction method.

Future Innovations

Future innovations refer to cutting-edge technologies currently in the research, development or early application stages that are expected to disrupt existing energy and resource utilization models within the next decade.

Why is it needed?:

Addresses the limitations of renewable energy,such as solar and wind, and offers ultimate zero-carbon energy solutions.
