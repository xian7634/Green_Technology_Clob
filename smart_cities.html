INTRODUCTION OF SMART CITY

Smart solutions for a greener, connected tomorrow.

DEFINITION

What is Smart City?

A Smart City is a new urban model that leverages IoT, Big Data, Cloud Computing, and AI as its technological foundation to digitally connect and intelligently coordinate subsystems such as transportation, buildings, energy, water, and governance.

Its goal is to achieve optimal resource allocation, minimal carbon emissions, and the highest quality of life for residents.

Smart Transportation

Utilizing AI, Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X), and real-time data analytics, it builds an efficient, safe, and low-carbon urban mobility system. It is one of the largest contributors to carbon reduction in a Smart City.

Example:

1. Adaptive signal control to dynamically adjusts traffic light timing based on real-time flow, reducing idling emissions

2. Smart parking to guide drivers to empty spots via sensors and apps, cutting cruising emissions

Smart Buildings

Smart Buildings use Building Management Systems (BMS) to centrally control and optimize lighting, HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning), elevators, and security. With environmental sensors and occupancy detection, buildings can automatically adjust indoor conditions to ensure comfort while supplying energy on demand. Smart buildings are a critical component of urban energy efficiency, effectively reducing power waste and supporting the integration of distributed energy sources like rooftop solar panels.

Smart Energy

The Smart Grid, which enables two-way power flow and dynamic scheduling.

By integrating renewable energy sources such as solar and wind, combined with energy storage systems and user-side demand response, the smart energy system can balance grid load and increase the absorption rate of new energy.

Additionally, smart meters in homes and businesses provide real-time energy consumption data, helping users optimize their usage behaviors, thereby globally driving the urban energy system toward cleanliness and high efficiency.
